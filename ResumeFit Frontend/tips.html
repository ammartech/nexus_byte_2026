Resume Tips

Small wording changes, bigger results

A few practical adjustments can make the difference between a resume that gets skimmed past and one that gets a closer look. Here's where to start.

Quick wins

Practical writing tips

🎯

Lead with results, not duties

Instead of describing what your role involved, describe what changed because you were in it. "Reduced support response time by 30%" says more than "Responsible for handling support tickets."

🔁

Mirror the job description's language

If a posting asks for "project coordination" and your resume says "managing tasks," consider aligning your wording. This helps both automated systems and human readers see the match faster.

📐

Use a consistent, simple structure

Stick to standard section names like "Experience," "Education," and "Skills." Use the same date format throughout, and avoid tables or columns that can confuse automated parsing.

✂️

Trim what isn't relevant

A shorter resume focused on relevant experience is easier to scan than a long one that includes everything you've ever done. Tailor each version to the role.

🔢

Add numbers wherever you can

Percentages, amounts, team sizes, and time frames give your achievements scale. "Trained 5 new staff" is more concrete than "helped train staff."

Watch out for

Common resume mistakes

These issues come up often and are usually quick to fix.

Generic summaries

"Hardworking professional seeking opportunities" tells a reader nothing specific. A summary should name your field, your strongest skill, and what you're aiming for next.

Inconsistent formatting

Mixed fonts, inconsistent spacing, or unpredictable bullet styles can make a resume look unfinished, even when the content is strong.

Missing contact details

A resume without a clear email, phone number, or location can cause a recruiter to move on rather than search for how to reach you.

At a glance

Dos and don'ts

✓ Do

  • Tailor your resume for each role you apply to.
  • Use clear section headings like "Experience" and "Skills."
  • Quantify achievements with numbers where possible.
  • Keep formatting simple and consistent throughout.
  • Proofread carefully, or ask someone else to review it.

✕ Don't

  • Use the exact same resume for every application.
  • Rely on images, tables, or columns for key information.
  • Pad your resume with unrelated jobs or skills.
  • List duties without mentioning outcomes or impact.
  • Use an email address that looks unprofessional.
